Network strategic planning and capacity

VISN 17 serves a large and shifting Veteran population across Texas. Capacity has to be planned years ahead of the demand it serves, and the analysis behind those decisions has to survive budget scrutiny across multiple fiscal years.

I took the role in an acting capacity as Chief Planning Officer before being appointed to it permanently. I direct the network's strategic planning functions, coordinate clinical restructuring and bed control protocols, and co-chair the strategic planning subcommittee. I guide the network's quadrennial market assessment, and produce the underlying analysis: demographic mapping, workload projection, space needs assessment, and business case analyses supporting multi-year program goals.

2024 – 2025
GHATP · VISN 17
ACHE presentation

Expanding ketamine administration routes for treatment-resistant depression

The network operated an esketamine nasal spray clinic. Nasal esketamine carries REMS requirements, observation time, and cost characteristics that limit how many Veterans a clinic can serve, while intravenous and intramuscular racemic ketamine offer a different operational and cost profile for the same clinical population.

I analyzed expanding the existing clinic's administration routes to include IV and IM racemic ketamine, and presented the findings to the American College of Healthcare Executives.

Rebuilding volunteer recruitment and placement

At the Center for Development and Civic Engagement I supervised volunteer training, scheduling, and placement across clinical and administrative areas, having started in the same office as a volunteer myself.

I developed proposals for a revised new-volunteer orientation, a placement-matching approach, and volunteer surveys feeding process improvement, and ran strategic communication initiatives to lift recruitment and engagement. I analyzed program trends through data compilation and reporting.
