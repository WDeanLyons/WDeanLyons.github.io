I plan capacity for a VA network serving Veterans across Texas — which comes down to one recurring question: where will demand be in five years, and what has to be built, staffed, or contracted now to meet it?

How I got here

I spent twelve years in the Army as a missile system electronic guidance technician, running a repair shop and a multi-vehicle motor pool as a section sergeant, and later leading a platoon and several electronic maintenance shops as an officer cadet. I was awarded the Combat Action Badge during Operation Iraqi Freedom. Alongside that I certified as a Level 2 instructor in the Modern Army Combatives Program, teaching hand-to-hand training to 101st Airborne and 5th Special Forces Group units at Fort Campbell.

I came into VA the way a lot of Veterans do: as a volunteer. I spent eight months in administration at the Center for Development and Civic Engagement at the Central Arkansas VA Health Care System, building an inventory room workflow and helping run events for local Veterans, before being selected into the Technical Career Field program as a Voluntary Services Specialist there. I have since logged more than 600 hours of volunteer service to Veterans. From there I was selected as a Graduate Healthcare Administrative Training Program fellow at the VISN 17 Network Office, and stayed on afterward in strategic planning.

What I work on

At network level the questions are about geography and time rather than individual clinics. Which counties are gaining Veterans and which are losing them. Whether projected workload justifies new space, new staffing, or a community care arrangement. What a program costs across a multi-year horizon, and what happens if it is not funded.

I have daily responsibility for coordinating strategic, capital, and market planning across the network's seven health care systems, working with facility planners, VISN leadership, and national program offices to align local initiatives with VHA priorities. I co-chair the Strategic Planning Subcommittee and oversee clinical restructuring, facility bed allocations, VA Site Tracking, and the Market Area Health Systems Optimization effort.

Underneath all of it is the same analytical work: demographic mapping, workload projection, space needs assessment, and the business case analyses that turn a projected gap into a funded plan. I came into the role in an acting capacity as Chief Planning Officer and was appointed to it permanently; the position was retitled afterward.

Separately from my paid role, I serve as a volunteer voting member of the Institutional Review Board at the Central Arkansas VA Health Care System, reviewing study protocols against the standards that protect the Veterans who enroll in them.
